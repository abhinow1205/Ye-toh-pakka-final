What 2.4 billion events taught us about scale.

The infrastructure decisions that let us process a week of usage data without anyone noticing we were under the hood.

Sam RuizStaff Engineer, Data Platform
Aug 1, 2026 10 min read
The week everything doubled Where the pipeline breaks Three changes that mattered What we'd do differently

Three weeks ago, one customer's usage doubled overnight. Not gradually — overnight. By the time we noticed the graph moving, we were already the busiest hour we'd ever had, and it kept climbing for six more days. This is what that week taught us about the difference between a system that scales on paper and one that scales at 2 a.m.

The week everything doubled

Our ingestion pipeline was built to comfortably absorb 3x our average load. We'd tested it, we'd load-tested it again, and we felt good about the headroom. What we hadn't tested was sustained 4x load for a hundred and sixty-eight hours straight, from a single tenant, with no warning.

The first symptom wasn't a crash. It was worse — everything kept working, just a little slower, in a way that took two days to notice and would have taken two more to diagnose if we hadn't already been watching the right dashboard.

"The system didn't fail. It just got quietly, patiently worse, until 140ms became 900ms and nobody had noticed the slope."

Where the pipeline actually breaks

Under normal load, our event pipeline looks almost boringly simple: ingest, validate, enrich, write. Under sustained pressure, the enrichment stage — the part that looks up account and session context for every single event — became the bottleneck. It wasn't the database. It was the connection pool sitting in front of it, sized for a load we no longer had.

That last number is the one we're proudest of. Slow is recoverable. Lost is not — and the enrichment queue, for all its faults, never dropped a single event. It just made everyone wait longer than we'd like to admit.

Three changes that mattered

We made a dozen small changes that week. Three of them accounted for almost all of the recovery:

1. Dynamic pool sizing. The enrichment connection pool now scales with observed queue depth instead of a fixed config value nobody remembers setting.

2. Per-tenant backpressure. One account's spike can no longer degrade latency for everyone else on the same shard.

3. A dashboard that would have caught this on day one. The slope, not just the current value — because 140ms creeping to 200ms over six hours looks fine on any single chart refresh.

// simplified: backpressure check before enrichment if (tenant.queueDepth > tenant.softLimit) { await shed(event, { reason: 'tenant_backpressure' }); return; }

What we'd do differently

Honestly? Not much about the response — the team handled a bad week well. What we'd change is upstream: load-test for duration, not just peak, and build the slope-detection dashboard before you need it, not during the incident it would have prevented.

2.4 billion events later, the system is faster and more honest about its limits than it was a month ago. That's usually how it goes.
